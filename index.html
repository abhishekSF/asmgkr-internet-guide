Common threats

Critical

Weak default passwords

Many ISP routers ship with a default admin login printed on the sticker. Anyone on your WiFi can log in and change your settings.

High

WPS PIN brute force

The 8-digit WPS PIN can be cracked in hours on many routers, giving attackers WiFi access even with a strong password.

High

Outdated firmware

Known vulnerabilities in old firmware let attackers take over routers remotely. ISP-supplied routers are often never updated.

High

Evil twin hotspots

Fake hotspots copying your network name trick devices into connecting so traffic and logins can be intercepted.

Medium

Insecure smart devices

Cheap cameras, plugs and TV boxes with weak security can be hijacked and used to reach your other devices.

Medium

Packet sniffing

On open or WEP-protected networks, anyone nearby can read unencrypted traffic.
