WiFi Security Guide

Threats to watch for, and the steps to lock down your home network.

Common Threats

Critical

Weak Default Passwords

Most routers ship with "admin/admin". Anyone nearby can log in and take over your network.

High

WPS PIN Brute Force

The 8-digit WPS PIN can be cracked in hours, giving attackers WiFi access even with a strong password.

High

Outdated Firmware

Known vulnerabilities in old firmware let hackers execute code on your router remotely.

High

Evil Twin Attacks

Fake hotspots mimicking your network name intercept your traffic and steal credentials.

Medium

IoT Device Exploitation

Smart cameras and speakers with weak security can be hijacked to attack your network.

Medium

Packet Sniffing

On unencrypted networks, attackers can read your passwords and messages in plain text.

Security Checklist

Do this

Change Admin Password

Set a unique 12+ character password for your router's admin panel immediately after setup.

Do this

Use WPA3 or WPA2-AES

Never use WEP or WPA/TKIP. WPA3 is best. WPA2-AES is acceptable if WPA3 isn't available.

Do this

Update Firmware Monthly

Check for updates regularly or enable auto-update. This patches known security holes.

Do this

Disable WPS

Turn off WiFi Protected Setup in router settings. It's convenient but fundamentally insecure.

Do this

Set Up a Guest Network

Put visitors and smart-home devices on a separate network so they can't access your main devices.

Do this

Rename Your SSID

Don't use your name, address, or ISP name. A generic name makes you a harder target.

Do this

Disable Remote Management

Only allow router admin access from inside your home network, not from the internet.

Do this

Monitor Connected Devices

Check your router's admin panel periodically for unknown devices. Use the Fing app to scan.

Advanced Tips

Use Secure DNS

Configure your router to use Cloudflare (1.1.1.1) or Google (8.8.8.8) with DNS-over-HTTPS to prevent DNS snooping.

Disable UPnP

Universal Plug and Play can be exploited by malware to open ports. Disable it and forward ports manually if needed.

Reduce Transmit Power

In apartments, lower your router's signal strength so it doesn't reach neighboring networks.

Enable Router Firewall

Most routers have a built-in SPI firewall. Make sure it's turned on to block unauthorized connections.
